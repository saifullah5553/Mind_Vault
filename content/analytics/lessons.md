# What the numbers say (read before choosing a topic and writing)

The daily run updates this file from `storage/analytics/latest.md`
(`.venv\Scripts\python -m scripts.analytics_report`). Each lesson names its evidence.
Under ~100 views a video, differences are mostly chance: keep a lesson only while it
keeps repeating, and mark it "tentative" until it does.

## 2026-11-13 (first week: 25 uploads, about 600 views, 1 subscriber)

1. **Shorts are the channel's front door.** 21 Shorts took 97% of all views; the four long
   videos took 13 views between them. Treat every reel as a film of its own: its first
   line and headline matter as much as the long video's title.
2. **Conflict, loss and reversal beat description (tentative, repeats 4 times).** Top
   Shorts: "How Iceland Lost Its Althing, Then Won It Back" (130 views, 8 likes, the best
   like rate), "The Night the North Sea Broke the Dutch Dikes" (130), "Why Your Hobby Died
   the Moment It Became Your Job" (73), "A 15-Year-Old Emperor and the Boshin War" (41).
   Bottom: "A Parliament Born in a Rift" (2), "Japan's Leaders Sail to See the West" (1),
   "Tsuda Umeko, Japan's First Railway and the Tomioka Silk Mill" (0), "The Maeslant
   Barrier" (6). Write reel headlines and titles around a turn: someone loses, breaks,
   is forced, fights back, wins it back. Lists of names ("X, Y and Z") do worst.
3. **A reel must stand on its own from its first word (tentative).** Parts that open
   mid-thought ("It was only the start", "They also hoped…") sit in the bottom third.
   Choose cut points where a new turn starts, and use `--parts` to move them.
4. **Part 1 averages 52 views, later parts 23.** The story's opening is the strongest
   reel; later parts need their own hook to survive without it.
5. **Long videos are not yet found by anyone.** Until retention data is available, keep
   the long video as the full story but put the effort into title + thumbnail curiosity
   ("Why…", "How… lost…", a number, a turn) and into reels that send viewers to it.

Missing: retention, average view percentage, shares and subscribers per video need the
YouTube Analytics API enabled in the Google Cloud project (owner action).

## 2026-11-16 (1,135 views, 2 subscribers; retention data now available)

6. **The first seconds decide a Short (repeats in 10 of 12 retention curves).** The
   steepest drop is before 10–20% of every reel but one. The headline and the first
   spoken line must carry the hook; open on the turn, never on set-up.
7. **Lesson 2 revised: "lists of names do worst" is contradicted.** Counts on 13 Nov
   were taken when the Japan reels were a day old. Three days later "Tsuda Umeko,
   Japan's First Railway and the Tomioka Silk Mill" has 56 views and "Japan's Leaders
   Sail to See the West" 55. Compare reels only at a similar age (views per day).
   Conflict and reversal still lead (North Sea 130, Unequal Treaties Ended 79, Hobby
   Died 73, Lost Its Althing 66): keep lesson 2's first part, still tentative.
8. **Part 1 still leads: 57 views on average, later parts 33 (repeats, 3 series).**
   Exception: Iceland Part 1 ("A Parliament Born in a Rift", 3 views) — a descriptive
   headline with no turn, which fits lesson 2.
9. **Long videos keep 8–18% of viewers on average (4 videos, tentative).** Viewers leave
   early; the cold open and first 30 seconds must show the strongest picture and state
   the twist, and the title must promise a turn the video delivers.

## 2026-11-18 (1,193 views, 2 subscribers, 31 Shorts)

10. **Lesson 2 repeats again: a turn or ending beats set-up.** The top Short is now a
    last part, "Meiji Japan: How the Unequal Treaties Finally Ended" (140 views, 29/day),
    ahead of every Part 1. "End of the Samurai" (80) and "Lost Its Althing, Then Won It
    Back" (66) also sit near the top. Descriptive headlines ("A Parliament Born in a
    Rift", 3; "The Maeslant Barrier", 9) stay at the bottom. Lesson 2 is no longer
    tentative: five series show it.
11. **Part 1 still leads on average (41 vs 30 views, 4 series), but a strong later part
    can beat it.** Give every part its own turn in the headline; never write a later part
    off as "continuation".
12. **Rosetta and Pavlov reels are 1–2 days old:** judge them only by views per day at
    the next run. Pavlov Part 1 ("Why His Biographer Says There Was No Bell", a myth
    reversal) runs at 26/day, the best start since Japan (tentative).
