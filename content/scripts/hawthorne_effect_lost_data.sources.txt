The Hawthorne Works was a large factory complex of the Western Electric Company in Cicero, Illinois, operating 1905-1983 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_Works
At its peak Hawthorne employed up to 45,000 workers | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_Works
Western Electric was the primary manufacturer of telephone equipment for the Bell System and a subsidiary of AT&T for most of its life | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Electric
The illumination experiments ran from 1924 to 1927 in two rooms, an experiment room and a control room | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
When light was increased productivity improved in both rooms; when light was decreased the result was the same; productivity in the experiment room only began to decrease when light was reduced to about the level of moonlight | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
In 1927 two female workers were chosen and they chose four other women; the team worked in a separate room; output was measured by counting finished relays dropped down a chute | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Two 5-minute breaks raised productivity, two 10-minute breaks raised it, six 5-minute breaks lowered it; soup or coffee with a sandwich in the morning and snacks later raised it; ending the day at 4:30 instead of 5:00 and dropping Saturday work raised it | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Changing a variable usually increased productivity, even if the change was back to the original condition | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Elton Mayo was born in Adelaide in 1880 and was appointed professor of industrial research at Harvard Business School in 1926 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_Mayo
Roethlisberger, Mayo's graduate assistant, and William J. Dickson of Western Electric did the bulk of the research; Mayo rarely visited the plant | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_Mayo
Mayo attributed the effect to the workers reacting to the sympathy and interest of the observers | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Mass interviewing ran from 1928 to 1930 with about 20,000 interviews; the bank wiring study (1931-1932) watched fourteen men whose output fell for fear the rate would be lowered | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Roethlisberger and Dickson published Management and the Worker (Harvard University Press, 1939), later voted the tenth most influential management book of the 20th century by Academy of Management Fellows | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Roethlisberger
The term Hawthorne effect was coined in the 1950s (1953, John R. P. French); Henry Landsberger (Hawthorne Revisited, 1958) suggested the novelty of being research subjects and increased attention could cause temporary increases | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
The Hawthorne effect is people changing their behaviour when they are aware of being observed; it is discussed for clinical trials (slightly better care, adherence) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
The illumination data were never formally analyzed and were thought destroyed; Levitt and List uncovered them; "existing descriptions of supposedly remarkable data patterns prove to be entirely fictional"; "hints of more subtle manifestations of a Hawthorne effect" (NBER Working Paper 15016, May 2009) | https://www.nber.org/papers/w15016
The data were found on microfilm at the University of Wisconsin in Milwaukee; List has said he remains confident the effect is genuine | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Parsons argued the participants had access to the counters of their work rate (feedback); Richard Nisbett called the story "a glorified anecdote" | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
The test-room study is described as methodologically poor and uncontrolled; Parsons found the studies too confounded | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_Mayo
The Hawthorne Works closed in 1983 and was replaced with a shopping center; one of the original towers remains at 22nd Street and Cicero Avenue | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_Works
The Hawthorne Works commissioned a study to see if its workers would become more productive in higher or lower levels of light | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_effect
Henry A. Landsberger reinterpreted the Hawthorne data (Hawthorne Revisited, 1958) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_Works
