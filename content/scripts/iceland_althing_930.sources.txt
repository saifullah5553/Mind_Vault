The Althing was founded in 930 at Thingvellir (Þingvellir, "assembly fields"); it is often described as one of the oldest parliaments in the world | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Althing
Thingvellir is a rift valley (graben) on the boundary of the North American and Eurasian plates, which are moving apart; the national park was founded in 1930 to mark the Althing's 1000th anniversary; UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Eingvellir
Thingvellir National Park, where the Althing met from 930 to 1798, was inscribed on the World Heritage List in 2004 | https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1152
The Landnámabók (Book of Settlements), known from later versions, names Ingólfr Arnarson as the first permanent settler and dates his arrival to 874 | https://www.britannica.com/place/Iceland/History
Ingólfr threw his high-seat pillars overboard and settled where they washed ashore, naming the place Reykjavík ("smoke bay") after the steam of hot springs | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ing%C3%B3lfr_Arnarson
Written sources stress settlers from Norway; genetic evidence shows founders from Scandinavia, Ireland and Scotland; Landnámabók suggests all usable land was taken within about sixty years | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_of_Iceland
The Althing met for two weeks every June; attendees lived in temporary booths (búðir) with walls of turf and rock | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_Commonwealth
The lawspeaker was elected for three years and recited one third of the law each year from the Law Rock (Lögberg), so the whole code was heard during a term; the law was later written down (1117-1118) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawspeaker
The Icelandic Commonwealth (930-1262) was a stateless commonwealth with no king and no executive body to enforce the law; enforcing a verdict fell to the injured party or his family | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_Commonwealth
Around 999/1000 the Althing was split between Christians and pagans who threatened to live by separate laws; the pagan lawspeaker Thorgeir lay under a fur cloak for a day and a night, then ruled that all should be Christian under one law, warning that dividing the law would divide the peace; private pagan worship and some old customs were allowed | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorgeir_Ljosvetningagodi
In the 13th century (the Age of the Sturlungs) powerful families fought for control of Iceland | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_the_Sturlungs
In 1262-1264 Iceland's chieftains accepted the rule of the king of Norway in the Old Covenant | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Covenant_(Iceland)
Iceland later came, with Norway, under the Danish crown | https://www.britannica.com/place/Iceland/History
The Althing met at Thingvellir until 1798 | https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1152
The Althing was disbanded by royal decree in 1800 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Althing
In 1845 a restored Althing met for the first time, in Reykjavík rather than Thingvellir; in 1918 Iceland became a separate kingdom in union with Denmark; on 17 June 1944 the republic was founded at Thingvellir with Sveinn Björnsson as first president, now celebrated as National Day | https://www.britannica.com/place/Iceland/Modern-Iceland
In the May 1944 referendum, about 98-99 percent voted to end the union with Denmark and found a republic | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_the_Republic_of_Iceland
The Althing was also a gathering for trade, marriages and news as well as law | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Eingvellir
In 1918 the Danish-Icelandic Act of Union made Iceland a separate kingdom in personal union with the Danish king | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iceland
The Almannagjá gorge at Thingvellir marks the edge of the North American plate and visitors walk through it | https://perlan.is/articles/thingvellir-national-park-iceland
