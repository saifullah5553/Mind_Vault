Perry's squadron of four ships (Susquehanna, Mississippi, Plymouth, Saratoga) reached Uraga, near Edo, in Edo Bay on 8 July 1853; Susquehanna and Mississippi were steam warships, Plymouth and Saratoga sailing sloops; the arrival is known as kurofune raiko, the arrival of the black ships | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Expedition
Perry, under orders from President Millard Fillmore, carried a letter from the President of the United States and promised to return for a reply | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Expedition
Perry returned in February 1854 with eight warships; the Convention of Kanagawa was signed on 31 March 1854 at Kanagawa (Yokohama) and opened the ports of Shimoda and Hakodate to American vessels | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Kanagawa
Sakoku: the policy of strictly limited foreign relations took shape in edicts of 1633 to 1639 and lasted more than 200 years; the only European trade allowed was the Dutch factory at Dejima; trade with China ran through the port of Nagasaki | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku
Dejima was an artificial island off Nagasaki where the Dutch traders lived | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima
The Tokugawa shogunate ruled Japan from Edo; the imperial court was in Kyoto | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate
The Harris Treaty (Treaty of Amity and Commerce), signed 29 July 1858, opened Kanagawa and four other ports to trade, fixed low import and export duties and granted extraterritoriality (Americans tried by consular courts); the 1858-era treaties are known as unequal treaties | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Amity_and_Commerce_(United_States%E2%80%93Japan)
The Ansei Treaties of 1858 were signed with the United States, Great Britain, Russia, the Netherlands and France | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansei_Treaties
The treaties were vehemently opposed in samurai quarters and the shogunate's apparent weakness led its opponents to call for its replacement (sonno joi) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonn%C5%8D_j%C5%8Di
Tokugawa Yoshinobu, the last shogun, tendered his resignation in November 1867; the restoration of imperial rule is dated 3 January 1868 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration
Emperor Meiji, personal name Mutsuhito, was born 3 November 1852 (so 15 in January 1868), reigned from 1867 until his death on 29 July 1912; Meiji means "enlightened rule"; Edo has been known as Tokyo since 1868 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Meiji
Boshin War, 27 January 1868 to 27 June 1869, between shogunate forces and the imperial court; result: imperial victory | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boshin_War
Tokyo means "eastern capital"; Edo was renamed Tokyo in 1868 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo
In 1871 the han (domains) were abolished and replaced by 3 fu and 302 ken prefectures; the following year these were merged into 72 prefectures | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_han_system
Iwakura Mission: led by Iwakura Tomomi with vice-ambassadors Okubo Toshimichi, Kido Takayoshi and Ito Hirobumi; 48 officials and 53 students and attendants; left Yokohama 23 December 1871, arrived San Francisco 15 January 1872, visited the US, Britain, France, Germany and other European countries, returned 13 September 1873; aimed to renegotiate the unequal treaties (unsuccessful) and to study industrial, political, military and educational systems | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwakura_Mission
Five young women went with the mission to study in the US, including the 6-year-old Tsuda Umeko | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwakura_Mission
Tsuda Umeko founded Joshi Eigaku Juku in 1900, now Tsuda University | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuda_University
Okubo Toshimichi was a samurai of the Satsuma Domain | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckubo_Toshimichi
Kido Takayoshi was born in Hagi in the Choshu Domain and helped form the Satsuma-Choshu (Satcho) Alliance of 1866 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kido_Takayoshi
Ito Hirobumi, from Choshu, was Japan's first prime minister (from 1885) and oversaw the drafting of the 1889 constitution | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%C5%8D_Hirobumi
Japan's first railway, Shimbashi to Yokohama, opened in 1872, built with British financing and British and European technical advisors; British engineer Edmund Morel supervised its construction | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transport_in_Japan
Tomioka Silk Mill: established in 1872 by the government to introduce modern machine silk reeling from France; Paul Brunat imported 300 reeling machines; silk was Japan's main export (86% of exports in 1862); UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomioka_Silk_Mill
Universal military conscription was instituted in 1873; the Haito edict of 28 March 1876 prohibited carrying weapons (swords) in public, except for the military and police (and former lords) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hait%C5%8Drei
The Meiji reforms brought the commutation and abolition of the samurai's hereditary stipends | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai
The Satsuma Rebellion of 1877, led by Saigo Takamori, was the last and most serious of the samurai uprisings and was fought against conscript soldiers of the new government | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satsuma_Rebellion
Meiji Constitution promulgated 11 February 1889, based mainly on the German and partly on the British model; the first Imperial Diet, with an elected House of Representatives, met in 1890 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Constitution
The Anglo-Japanese Treaty of Commerce and Navigation was signed on 16 July 1894 and came into force in 1899, after which British subjects came under Japanese courts | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Japanese_Treaty_of_Commerce_and_Navigation
Extraterritoriality in Japan was fully ended in 1899 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unequal_treaty
