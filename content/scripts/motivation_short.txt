TITLE: Why Your Hobby Died the Moment It Became Your Job
DESCRIPTION: In 1971, one experiment showed how a reward can quietly kill what you love.
THUMBNAIL: WHY YOUR PASSION DIED

[INTRODUCTION]
This is why your passion died.

In 1971, psychologist Edward Deci gave students a puzzle they loved to solve. Some were paid for every solution. Others got nothing.

Then the money stopped. He left them alone, and watched in secret.

The students who were never paid kept playing. The students who had been paid? They spent less time on it.

Two years later, it happened with preschoolers. Children who loved drawing were promised an award. Afterward, they drew about half as much.

Reward something people love, and it can start to feel like work.

So take the reason back. Choose one thing you used to love. Give it twenty minutes this week. No audience. No goal. No score.
