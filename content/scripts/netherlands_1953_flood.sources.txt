The North Sea flood struck the Netherlands on the night of 31 January to 1 February 1953, a Saturday night, when a storm coincided with a high spring tide | https://www.britannica.com/event/North-Sea-flood
1,836 people died in the Netherlands; the south-west (Zeeland, South Holland) was worst hit; many were not warned in time | https://www.watersnoodmuseum.nl/en/the-flood
Tens of thousands (about 70,000) were evacuated; 307 died in England and 22 in Belgium | https://marineinfo.org/doc/publication/201593
About 26 percent of the Netherlands lies below sea level and about 55 to 60 percent is at risk of flooding from sea or rivers | https://www.pbl.nl/en/correction-wording-flood-risks-for-the-netherlands-in-ipcc-report
The Beemster polder, a lake north of Amsterdam drained in the early 17th century, is a model of Dutch land reclamation and a World Heritage Site | https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/899
The Kinderdijk mills were built 1738-1740 to drain the land; the Mill Network at Kinderdijk-Elshout was inscribed by UNESCO in 1997 | https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/818
The 1916 flood led to adoption of Cornelis Lely's plan; the Afsluitdijk (about 30 km) was completed in 1932 and turned the Zuiderzee into the freshwater IJsselmeer, from which new polders were drained | https://www.britannica.com/topic/Zuiderzee-project
The last breach, at Ouwerkerk, was closed on the night of 6 to 7 November 1953 with four Phoenix caissons (wartime floating harbour units built in England); they now house the Watersnoodmuseum | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouwerkerk
The caissons at Ouwerkerk house the Watersnoodmuseum, the national knowledge and remembrance centre for the 1953 flood | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watersnoodmuseum
The Delta Committee was set up in 1953 after the flood; its plan closed the sea inlets of the south-west, shortening the coastline, with safety standards of up to one in 10,000 years | https://open.rws.nl/publish/pages/48106/final_report_1_report_of_the_delta_committee.pdf
The Delta Works were completed in 1986; the last barrier was across the Eastern Scheldt; dams carry road connections to the former islands | https://www.britannica.com/event/Delta-Works
After protests to protect the estuary's ecology, the Eastern Scheldt barrier was built with gates that stay open and close only in storms; it opened in October 1986 | https://www.watersnoodmuseum.nl/en/water-knowledge/learn-about-water-safety/articles/eastern-scheldt-storm-surge-barrier
The Maeslant barrier near Rotterdam (completed 1997) has two gates stored in dry docks on each bank; it closes automatically, decided by computer, when water is forecast above 3 m NAP at Rotterdam | https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/en/projects/iconic-structures/maeslant-barrier
The Netherlands is often described as the safest delta in the world | https://www.climatechangepost.com/netherlands/coastal-floods/
