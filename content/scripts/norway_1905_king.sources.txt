Under the Treaty of Kiel (January 1814) the king of Denmark-Norway gave Norway up to the king of Sweden | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kiel
Norway refused the treaty, declared independence and convened a constituent assembly at Eidsvoll; 112 elected representatives met there in April 1814 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Norway_(1814)
The Eidsvoll constitution was signed on 17 May 1814 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Constituent_Assembly
Norway celebrates 17 May every year as Constitution Day, its national day | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Day_(Norway)
Swedish troops marched into Norway on 29 July 1814 and quickly put down resistance; the Convention of Moss ceasefire was signed on 14 August 1814 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Moss
The Swedish king accepted the Eidsvoll constitution, amended only for the union | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Moss
In the union Norway kept its constitution, parliament, courts, legal system, armed forces, flag and currency; it shared the monarch and a foreign policy run by the Swedish foreign ministry | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Christian Michelsen, a Bergen shipowner, became prime minister in March 1905 (11 March 1905) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Michelsen
Michelsen's government's only stated objective was a separate Norwegian corps of consuls | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
The Storting passed the Consular Act in late May 1905 | https://www.kongehuset.no/artikkel.html?tid=30095
On 27 May 1905 King Oscar refused to sign the bill; the ministers refused to countersign and resigned collectively, in Stockholm | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Michelsen
Oscar refused to accept the resignations, as he could not form a new government | https://www.regjeringen.no/en/the-government/previous-governments/regjeringer-siden-1814/historiske-regjeringer/ministries-1905---1940/christian-michelsens-government-1905---1/id507098
Michelsen and his ministers returned to Christiania (Kristiania); on 7 June 1905 the Storting unanimously declared the union dissolved, "since the constitutional monarchy has ceased to exist", and told the resigned cabinet to exercise the king's powers | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
The Swedish government insisted on a Norwegian plebiscite; Norway had already set it for 13 August 1905 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Referendum of 13 August 1905: 368,208 for dissolution, 184 against; only men could vote | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Norwegian_union_dissolution_referendum
Women collected more than 200,000 signatures for dissolution (279,878 per Wikipedia; 244,765 per the Royal House; script says "more than two hundred thousand") | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Troops were quietly deployed on both sides of the border, in places 2 km apart; Swedish Social Democrats opposed calling up reserves and a war | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Karlstad negotiations began 31 August 1905 and concluded 23 September 1905; the Storting accepted on 9 October and the Riksdag on 13 October | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
The Karlstad agreement set a neutral zone on the frontier and required the demolition of Norwegian forts in it (with some kept) | https://archive.spectator.co.uk/article/30th-september-1905/2/the-anticipations-of-a-satisfactory-settlement-bet
Under the Karlstad Treaty the Ørje forts were taken out of service and demolished in 1905, and the guns moved to Hegra | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%98rje_Fortress
On 26 October 1905 Oscar II renounced the Norwegian throne and Sweden recognised Norway's independence | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Prince Carl was the second son of Crown Prince Frederik (later Frederick VIII) of Denmark; his wife Maud was the youngest daughter of the future King Edward VII; their son Alexander (later Olav) was born 2 July 1903 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII
Carl would accept the crown only if a referendum showed the people wanted a monarchy and the Storting then elected him | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Monarchy referendum 12-13 November 1905: 259,563 (78.94%) for monarchy, 69,264 (21.06%) for a republic | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Norwegian_monarchy_referendum
The Storting elected Carl on 18 November 1905; he accepted that evening, took the name Haakon, and his son became Olav | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Norwegian_monarchy_referendum
Haakon VII was the first king after Norway's full independence in 1905, elected by the Storting on 18 November | https://www.britannica.com/biography/Haakon-VII
The family landed at Vippetangen in Christiania on 25 November 1905; Haakon arrived with Crown Prince Olav on his arm | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Haakon swore his oath to the constitution before the Storting on 27 November 1905 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_union_between_Norway_and_Sweden
Haakon and Maud were crowned on 22 June 1906 in Nidaros Cathedral, Trondheim | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1905_Norwegian_monarchy_referendum
Germany invaded Norway in April 1940; Haakon rejected German demands to legitimise the Quisling regime's puppet government, vowing to abdicate rather than do so, and went into exile in Great Britain | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII
The Norwegian government-in-exile was based in London | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII
Haakon returned to Norway in June 1945 after the defeat of Germany and died on 21 September 1957 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_VII
