The 1893 petition ("Mary J. Carpenter and 25,519 others") was presented to Parliament by Sir John Hall on 28 July 1893 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Women%27s_Suffrage_Petition
The petition is more than 500 sheets joined into a roll over 270 metres long; staff unwound part of it the length of the debating chamber | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Women%27s_Suffrage_Petition
The main petition holds 25,519 signatures; thirteen petitions in 1893 had 31,872 signatures in all; twelve of the smaller petitions have not survived | https://archives.govt.nz/discover-our-stories/womens-suffrage-petition
Signers were women aged 21 or older; at least 20 men also signed | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Women%27s_Suffrage_Petition
Petitions: over 9,000 signatures in 1891, almost 20,000 in 1892, nearly 32,000 in 1893, almost a quarter of the adult European female population | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand
Sheets were sent around the country and back to Christchurch, where Kate Sheppard pasted each sheet end on end and rolled it around a section of a broom handle | https://nzhistory.govt.nz/media/photo/suffrage-petition-1893
Kate Sheppard was born in Liverpool, England; her family emigrated to New Zealand in 1868; she died in Christchurch on 13 July 1934 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Sheppard
The campaign was run by the Women's Christian Temperance Union, which campaigned against alcohol; in 1887 Sheppard was appointed its National Superintendent for Franchise and Legislation | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Sheppard
Sheppard wrote the pamphlet Ten Reasons Why the Women of New Zealand Should Vote (1888) | https://my.christchurchcitylibraries.com/kate-sheppard/
Suffrage bills passed the House of Representatives in 1891 and 1892 but were defeated in the Legislative Council (the 1892 bill failed over a postal vote amendment) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand
Members of the Legislative Council were appointed, not elected | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Legislative_Council
Premier John Ballance, a supporter of women's suffrage, died in April 1893; Richard Seddon succeeded him and opposed the bill | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ballance
Seddon opposed women's suffrage because many women supported prohibition; he was close to the liquor trade; lobbyists for the liquor industry petitioned the upper house | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_New_Zealand_general_election
Henry Fish, MP for South Dunedin, opposed suffrage; counter-petitions gathered about 5,000 signatures | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Sheppard
Councillor Thomas Kelly had paired in favour; Seddon obtained his consent by wire (telegram) to change his vote; William Reynolds and Edward Stevens, incensed by Seddon's manipulation, changed sides and voted for the bill | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_New_Zealand_general_election
Suffragists gave supporters in Parliament white camellias to wear in their buttonholes | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand
The Legislative Council passed the bill 20 votes to 18 on 8 September 1893 | https://teara.govt.nz/en/voting-rights/page-4
Eighteen councillors petitioned the governor, Lord Glasgow, to withhold consent; he signed the Electoral Act on 19 September 1893 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand
New Zealand became the first self-governing country in the world where women could vote in parliamentary elections | https://nzhistory.govt.nz/politics/womens-suffrage/
The general election was on 28 November 1893; the Maori electorates voted on 20 December 1893; Maori women voted too | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_New_Zealand_general_election
About 65% of eligible New Zealand women voted in the 1893 election | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_New_Zealand_general_election
Elizabeth Yates was elected mayor of Onehunga in 1893 by 13 votes, the first woman in the British Empire elected mayor | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Yates
Women could not stand for Parliament until 1919; Elizabeth McCombs became the first woman MP in a 1933 by-election | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women%27s_suffrage_in_New_Zealand
In Britain the Representation of the People Act 1918 gave the vote to some women over 30 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act_1918
In the United States the 19th Amendment gave women the vote nationwide in 1920 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution
Sheppard was elected first president of the National Council of Women of New Zealand in 1896 | https://teara.govt.nz/en/biographies/2s20/sheppard-katherine-wilson
Sheppard's portrait replaced Queen Elizabeth II on the New Zealand ten-dollar note in 1991 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Sheppard
