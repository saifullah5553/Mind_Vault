Ivan Pavlov was born on 26 September 1849 in Ryazan; his father was a village Russian Orthodox priest; he entered the local theological seminary and abandoned his religious career without graduating | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov
In 1870 Pavlov enrolled in the physics and mathematics department at the University of Saint Petersburg to study natural science | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov
Ryazan is a city south-east of Moscow | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan
Saint Petersburg was the capital of the Russian Empire (1713-1918, with a short break) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg
In 1891 Pavlov was invited to the Institute of Experimental Medicine to organise and direct the Department of Physiology | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov
Pavlov won the 1904 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in recognition of his work on the physiology of digestion; he died on 27 February 1936 of double pneumonia, aged 86, in Leningrad | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov
He did not win his Nobel Prize (1904) for conditional reflexes but for digestive physiology; he first got a "real job" at age 41 | https://blog.oup.com/2014/11/ivan-pavlov-surprising-facts/
Pavlov redirected the animals' digestive fluids outside the body, where they could be measured; his dogs began to salivate in the presence of the technician who normally fed them; he called this anticipatory salivation "psychic secretion" | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_conditioning
Food is the unconditioned stimulus; the conditioned stimulus (e.g. the sound of a metronome) comes to produce salivation; extinction: the CS presented repeatedly without the US; also called Pavlovian conditioning; became the foundation of behaviourism | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_conditioning
Pavlov didn't use a bell, and for his real scientific purposes couldn't; "bell" comes from a mistranslation of zvonok (buzzer) | https://blog.oup.com/2014/11/ivan-pavlov-surprising-facts/
Pavlov's lab used electric shocks, whistles, metronomes, tuning forks and visual stimuli; his ideas spread through the writings of John B. Watson and B. F. Skinner; he was praised by Lenin and did not conceal his disapproval of Soviet Communism | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov
Todes (Johns Hopkins) found the bell could not be controlled precisely; the lab's main tools were the metronome, harmonium, buzzer and electric shock | https://hub.jhu.edu/magazine/2014/winter/daniel-todes-biography-of-pavlov/
Pavlov saw the conditional reflex as a tool for exploring the animal and human psyche; "Essentially, only one thing in life is of real interest to us" ... our psychical experience; dogs have personalities, emotions and thoughts; lab dogs were given names that captured their personalities | https://blog.oup.com/2014/11/ivan-pavlov-surprising-facts/
Pavlov seriously contemplated leaving Russia after the Bolshevik seizure of power in 1917; he was one of very few public critics of the Bolsheviks' political repression, yet praised the state for its great support of science; protested the repression of religion and supported the local church near his lab at Koltushi; was working on manuscripts when he died | https://blog.oup.com/2014/11/ivan-pavlov-surprising-facts/
Pavlov's best-known cases of experimentally induced neurosis resulted from a severe flood in Leningrad in 1924; a second method gave dogs particularly difficult conditioning tasks | https://www.taylorfrancis.com/books/9781351797603/chapters/10.4324%2F9781315206622-6
A monument to a dog sculpted by Bezpalov in 1935, with narrative bas-reliefs and texts written by I. P. Pavlov, stands at the Institute of Experimental Medicine, 12 Akademika Pavlova Street | https://encspb.ru/object/2855704461?lc=en
The dog monument was created on the initiative of Pavlov and honours the laboratory dogs used in his research | https://journals.eco-vector.com/MAJ/article/view/9681
Saint Petersburg was renamed Petrograd in 1914 and Leningrad in 1924 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg
Koltushi is a village just east of Saint Petersburg, site of Pavlov's biological station | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koltushi
John B. Watson was an American psychologist who popularised behaviorism | https://www.britannica.com/biography/John-B-Watson
