The Rosetta Stone was found in July 1799 by French army officer Pierre-François Bouchard during France's invasion of Egypt, at Fort Julien near the town of Rashid (Rosetta) in the Nile Delta, where it had been used as building material | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
Napoleon's expedition to Egypt (1798) was accompanied by a corps of 151 technical experts (savants); the find was reported to the Institut d'Égypte in Cairo | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
It was the first Ancient Egyptian bilingual text recovered in modern times; lithographic copies and plaster casts soon circulated among European museums and scholars | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The top and middle texts are in Ancient Egyptian in hieroglyphic and Demotic scripts, the bottom in Ancient Greek; the Greek text provided the starting point for decipherment | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
Under the Capitulation of Alexandria (1801) the stone passed to the British; it landed at Portsmouth in February 1802 aboard HMS Égyptienne and has been on display at the British Museum since 1802, where it is the most visited object | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
Painted on its sides: "Captured in Egypt by the British Army in 1801" and "Presented by King George III" | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The stone is granodiorite, 112.3 cm high, 75.7 cm wide, about 760 kg; wax and chalk gave it a dark colour that led to its mistaken identification as black basalt | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The first complete translation of the Greek text was published in 1803 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The decree was issued in 196 BC by a congress of priests at Memphis honouring Ptolemy V, who had been crowned at Memphis aged about 12 just over a year earlier; it concludes that a copy was to be placed in every temple | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
A priestly decree of 196 BC concerning Ptolemy V, issued in hieroglyphic, Demotic and Greek | https://www.worldhistory.org/Rosetta_Stone/
The stone is a fragment of a larger stele; only the last 14 lines of the hieroglyphic text survive, the Demotic has 32 lines and the Greek 54 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The last known hieroglyphic inscription is dated 24 August 394, at Philae; the closing of the temples led to the loss of fluent readers and writers of hieroglyphs | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs
In Europe it was widely believed that Egyptian scripts were exclusively ideographic, representing ideas rather than sounds | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decipherment_of_ancient_Egyptian_scripts
Thomas Young was an English physician (MD 1796) known for the wave theory of light; he began Egyptology in 1813 and by 1814 had translated the "enchorial" (Demotic) text | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Young_(scientist)
Young matched the name Ptolemy in the Greek to a hieroglyphic cartouche but believed cartouches were phonetic only for foreign names | https://www.thecollector.com/rosetta-stone-secrets-unlocked/
Young's memorial in Westminster Abbey: he "first penetrated the obscurity which had veiled for ages the hieroglyphicks of Egypt" | https://www.westminster-abbey.org/abbey-commemorations/commemorations/thomas-young
Jean-François Champollion was born 23 December 1790 at Figeac; he learned to read and speak Coptic and studied many languages in his teens | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_Champollion
In 1822 Champollion deciphered the names Ramses and Thutmose written in hieroglyphs in temple cartouches; he showed the writing system combined phonetic and ideographic signs | https://www.thecollector.com/rosetta-stone-secrets-unlocked/
On 14 September 1822, visiting his brother Jacques-Joseph, Champollion exclaimed "Je tiens l'affaire!" and then fainted; on 27 September 1822 he presented the Lettre à M. Dacier at the Académie in Paris | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettre_%C3%A0_M._Dacier
Young attended Champollion's presentation of 27 September 1822 | https://www.thecollector.com/rosetta-stone-secrets-unlocked/
Some faulted Champollion for not giving sufficient credit to Young's earlier discoveries; in 1824 he published the Précis; he travelled to Egypt (expedition left Toulon in July 1828) and died in Paris in 1832, aged 41 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_Champollion
In 1917 the museum, concerned about heavy bombing in London, moved the stone to a station of the Postal Tube Railway at Mount Pleasant near Holborn; it was cleaned in 1999, revealing dark grey rock with a pink vein | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
The stone was displayed for one month in October 1972 at the Louvre beside Champollion's Lettre; the term "Rosetta stone" now means the essential clue to a new field of knowledge | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone
